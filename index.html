01 — The gap

The gap is not youth energy. It is exclusion from the rooms where decisions are made.

National budgets, tax policies, leadership succession, security strategies, and governance reforms continue to be designed with little meaningful youth representation, despite young people being the demographic most affected by these decisions.

When young people are denied institutionalized avenues to influence policy, they turn to the streets and social media as alternative platforms. Kenya's 2024 Finance Bill — advanced with minimal youth consultation — produced the largest youth-led protest movement in the country's history.

Four pressures
01
Limited youth participation in governance and policy processes
Budgets, tax policy, security strategy and leadership succession are decided without meaningful youth representation.
02
Rising youth unemployment and marginalization
Nearly one in three working young people live below the international poverty line of $2.15 a day, deepening a sense of exclusion.
03
Increasing exposure to violent extremism and social fragmentation
The AU Peace and Security Council identifies high youth unemployment and poverty as the primary structural vulnerability exploited by extremist networks across the Sahel, Lake Chad Basin and Horn of Africa.
04
Weak capacity-building structures for ethical leadership
State programmes focus on short-term labour supply and neglect long-term capacity in public accountability and statecraft — no integrity hubs, no structured intergenerational mentorship.

03 — Strategy

How the work scales.

Grassroots localization
Capacity building
National leadership
Impact
01

The "Train-the-Leader" cascade model

Rather than reaching millions individually, TGen Africa intensively trains a cohort of high-potential young leaders from developing nations. Those leaders become ambassadors, creating an exponential growth model.

02

Strategic public-private partnerships

Peace and security initiatives are aligned with the CSR goals of private enterprise and the development agendas of the AU and UN, securing sustainable funding and institutional backing.

03

Digital mobilization and data-driven advocacy

Accessible mobile technology and data collection tools let youth report security insights, track development progress, and collaborate across borders in real time.
